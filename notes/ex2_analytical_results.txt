    NOTE: the 150 mm channel crosses the 60 mm chamber, so the
    channel volume inside the chamber footprint is 120 uL, but that is part of the chamber volume, not extra; the remaining 90 mm of channel (the 45 mm inlet and 45 mm outlet ducts) holds another 180 uL, so the system holds 3780 uL of fluid in all. The exercise (and the
    required sanity check) uses the CHAMBER volume alone.

A2  FILLING TIME AT Q = 100 uL/min
    Q = 100 uL/min = 1.666667e-09 m^3/s
    t_fill = V_chamber/Q = 2160.0000 s = 36.0000 min
    solute mass in the pulse = c*Q*t_fill = 3.600000e-06 mol = V_chamber*c exactly
    => choose the pulse 'filling time' parameter = t_fill = 2160 s:
       the chamber is just filled when the pulse ends. (Shorter
       pulses under-dose the far corners; longer ones only add
       solute that leaves through the outlet.)
    time-dependent study window: -5 s .. 3*t_fill = 6480 s

A3  REYNOLDS NUMBER, INLET CHANNEL

    Re = rho*U*D_h/eta = 1000*8.3333e-04*1.3333e-03/1e-03 = 1.1111
    -> Re = 1.111 << 2000: the flow is firmly LAMINAR.

A4  INLET-CHANNEL VELOCITY AND RESIDENCE TIME

    t_res = L/U = 180.0000 s = 3.000 min
    cross-check V_channel/Q = 180.0000 s

A5  DIFFUSION TIMES (D = 200 um^2/s)
    across the chamber height, h^2/(pi^2 D) : 5.0661e+02 s (8.44 min)
    across half the height,   h^2/(4 D)   : 1.2500e+03 s (20.83 min)
    along the chamber, L^2/D (L = 6 cm)   : 1.8000e+07 s (5000.0 h = 208.3 days)
    vs t_fill = 2160 s:  t_fill/t_diff,perp = 4.26,  t_diff,long/t_fill = 8333
    -> transverse (1 mm) homogenisation is ~4x FASTER than the
       flushing time, so the chamber mixes across its height;
       but diffusion along the 60 mm length is ~8000x SLOWER than
       the flushing time, so only the flow path is exchanged.

A6  EFFECTIVE PECLET NUMBER IN THE CHAMBER
    primary: flow cross-section A = 60 mm x 1 mm = 6.0000e-05 m^2
      U_chamber = Q/A = 2.777778e-05 m/s (27.78 um/s)
      advective traverse L/U = 2160.0000 s (= V_chamber/Q = t_fill)
      Pe = U_chamber*h/D = 138.889   (h = 1 mm)
      t_adv / t_diff,perp = 4.264
    variant: with the plan area 60 x 60 mm:
      U = 4.6296e-07 m/s, Pe = 2.315
    jet Pe in the 2 mm channel: U*h/D = 4167
    -> Pe >> 1: transport is advection dominated, i.e. the
       concentration is set by what ENTERS, not by diffusion.
       Two consequences:
       (i)  across the 1 mm height: t_diff,perp = 507 s is
           still 4.3x shorter than the residence time
           2160 s, so the flushed column stays vertically
           uniform (the inflow is already uniform at c = 1);
       (ii) along the 60 cm: t_diff,long = 1.8e+07 s =
           8333x the residence time, so lateral
           homogenisation by diffusion is hopeless - only the 2 mm
           wide flow path is exchanged and the rest of the
           footprint is a dead zone (this is the part-C effect).

PART B - DOMAIN-AVERAGE CONCENTRATION VS TIME (fluid exchange)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Pulse (Rect1): start = -5 s, end = t_fill = 2160 s,
    transition-zone size = 5, 2 continuous derivatives.
    NB: with a smoothing zone of 5 the effective ON/OFF edges sit
    at -5+2.5 = -2.5 s and t_fill-2.5 s, so the delivered dose is
    ~ (t_fill+2.5) s of full-strength inflow, i.e. ~1.001 x V_chamber of solute.
  Study: stationary step 1 (velocity) + time-dependent step 2
  (species, velocity taken from step 1), t from -5 s to
  3*t_fill = 6480 s, output step 5-10 s (use 5 s).
  Analytical brackets for the domain probe (chamber-average c):
    well-mixed (CSTR): tau = V/Q = 2160 s,
      c_avg(t_fill) = 1-exp(-1) = 0.6321 mol/m^3,
      c_avg(3*t_fill) = 0.6321*exp(-2) = 0.0855 mol/m^3;
    plug-flow limit: c_avg = min(t/t_fill, 1) -> 1.0 mol/m^3 at
      t_fill (upper bracket).
    The COMSOL probe should fall between the two brackets; a value
    well below the CSTR curve means the chamber corners are dead
    zones that are not flushed at all.
  Solute mass balance: Q*c*t_fill = 3.6000e-06 mol = V_chamber*c
    -> at best the chamber reaches c = 1 mol/m^3 if nothing is
    lost to the outlet and the mixing is perfect.

PART C - THREE POINT PROBES (homogeneity)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Suggested probe points (chamber footprint 60 x 60 mm, channel along
  x, centred on y = 0):
    P1 = (-29.5, 0, 0) mm  - upstream end, on the channel axis
    P2 = (0, 29.5, 0) mm   - off-axis, next to a side wall
    P3 = (29.5, 0, 0) mm   - downstream end, on the channel axis
  On-axis advective arrival (jet speed 0.833 mm/s):
    P1: 45.5 mm from the inlet -> 54.6 s
    P3: 105.5 mm from the inlet -> 126.6 s
  Off-axis P2: lateral gap from the channel edge (1 mm) = 28.5 mm,
    diffusion time = gap^2/D = 4.061e+06 s = 47.0 days
    -> utterly outside the 3*t_fill study window: the off-axis
       corner stays near c = 0 unless the FLOW reaches it.
  Transverse diffusion across the 1 mm height: 507 s, i.e.
  4.26 x SHORTER than the chamber residence time 2160 s:
    the column above the floor homogenises fast, but only the narrow
    strip along the channel axis is actually flushed -> large
    in-plane inhomogeneity, which is what part C should show.
  Meaning for cells on the floor: cells under the channel get the
  drug first and most; cells far from the axis get almost none.

PART D - HOW TO IMPROVE HOMOGENEITY (simulate one)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  (1) Serpentine / meander inlet that sweeps the whole 60 x 60 mm
      footprint, or several parallel inlet channels.
  (2) Shrink the chamber footprint until L^2/D ~ t_fill.
  (3) Raise Q (t_fill scales as 1/Q; Re stays << 2000).
  Recommended: option (1). t_diff,along = L^2/D = 1.80e+07 s is
  8333 x the residence time, so diffusion
  can never fill the far corners in this study - the flow must be
  brought to them. A meander keeps Re and t_fill unchanged but
  gives every point an advective residence time of O(t_fill).

PART E - TRANSIENT IN THE CELL DOMAIN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Cell-domain physics: zero velocity (Fluid domain condition),
  D = 0.7*Dsp = 1.4000e-10 m^2/s, reaction -k*c with
  k = 0.15 mol/m^3/s (15 %/s of the local concentration).
  Initial Values: c = 1 mol/m^3 in the inlet channel only (chamber
  starts at 0), inflow c = 1 mol/m^3 (no pulse), study from t = 0.
  Cell-layer relaxation (pinned surface): slowest mode
  tau = 1/(k + D_eff*(pi/2L_c)^2) = 5.42 s,
  t_90 = 18.7 s, t_99 = 31.2 s
  (1/k = 6.67 s dominates: reaction is much faster than
  diffusion across the layer, L_c^2/D_eff = 71.4 s).
  => the cell layer itself equilibrates within tens of seconds,
     but its SURFACE concentration only rises as the chamber fills
     (O(t_fill) = 2160 s), so the cell-domain curve in the
     COMSOL run follows the chamber flush and is flat only after
     roughly 2-3 t_fill = 4320-6480 s.
  Output saving: every 5 s up to t_fill, every 200 s afterwards
  (as suggested in the assignment).

PART F - STATIONARY STUDY, FLOW-RATE SWEEP
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Stationary step 2 (species) coupled to the stationary velocity of
  step 1; parametric sweep Q = 10 uL/min, 100 uL/min, 1 mL/min.
  Re over the sweep: 0.111, 1.111, 11.111  (all << 2000, laminar).
  Pinned-surface expectation: c_mid = 7.565625e-02 mol/m^3 for ALL
  three flow rates - the cell-layer profile is set by reaction and
  diffusion alone, not by the flow rate.
  How Q enters (the point of the comparison): through the
  chamber-side mass-transfer film. I use k_m ~ D_fluid/delta with
  the diffusion boundary-layer thickness estimated from the
  residence time, delta = sqrt(D_fluid * t_res) and t_res = V/Q,
  so k_m = sqrt(D_fluid*Q/V) and k_m ~ Q^(1/2).
  (The classical Leveque/Levich scaling for laminar channel flow is
   k_m ~ (D_f^2 U/(Dh*L))^(1/3) ~ U^(1/3) ~ Q^(1/3); it gives the
   same monotone trend. NOTE: the form quoted in the task brief,
   (D_f^2 U/(nu*L_ch))^(-1/3), is dimensionally inconsistent - the
   correct Leveque exponent is +1/3 and nu does not appear.)
  Qualitative statement: a HIGHER flow rate gives a thinner film,
  a larger k_m, a smaller film drop, hence a HIGHER steady-state
  concentration in the cell layer - 1 mL/min > 100 uL/min >
  10 uL/min (with the caveat that the chamber is not well mixed,
  so the real trend is steeper than the idealised model).

PART G - ARE ALL CELLS IN IDENTICAL CONDITIONS?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Inside one cell layer c varies from 1 mol/m^3 at the surface to
  0.0757 mol/m^3 at the impermeable base (factor 13.2):
  cells at different depths are NOT identical, and this gradient is
  intrinsic (reaction-limited), not a flow artefact.
  Laterally, the part-C inhomogeneity relaxes only by diffusion:
  sqrt(2 D t) = 5.9 mm per day, so
  a 60 mm dead zone needs ~104 days.
  Over a multi-day culture the lateral gradient is therefore only
  washed out if the flow keeps renewing the boundary layer; the
  reaction (1/k = 6.7 s) makes the supply chronically marginal.
  Verdict: cells growing on the floor over the whole 60 x 60 mm
  footprint are NOT in identical conditions; only cells within the
  swept strip (or under a meander inlet) see the same nutrient.
